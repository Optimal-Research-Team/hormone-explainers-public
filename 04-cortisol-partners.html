Cortisol's partners

Cortisol does not act alone. Some molecules turn down its production, some soften its effects once released, and some sit on the other end of the see-saw.

    The stress axis

    Cortisol is made at the end of a chain. The hypothalamus releases CRH, the pituitary releases ACTH, and the adrenal cortex releases cortisol, which then acts on tissues throughout the body.

    Its partners work at different points along this chain.

    Upstream

    Cortisol is its own brake

    Cortisol has its own negative feedback loop. Once the job is done, it switches off the signals upstream that called for it.

    Upstream · act on CRH, ACTH or the adrenal

    Turning production down

    Oxytocin
    Can ↓ ACTH and ↓ cortisol, especially under stress, and calms stress circuits in the brain. Clear in animal studies; human results are mixed.
    ANP
    A hormone released by the heart: ↓ CRH, ↓ ACTH and ↓ cortisol in studies.
    Beta-endorphin
    One of the body’s own opioids: ↓ ACTH, ↓ cortisol.
    Upstream

    Quieting the drive

    GABA
    Quiets the hypothalamic drive.
    Endocannabinoids
    Dampen HPA activation.
    Melatonin
    Opposes cortisol across the day–night cycle.
    Downstream

    Buffering the effect

    Cortisol is still released, but some of its downstream actions are offset. DHEA and testosterone tend to build tissue up where cortisol breaks it down, which is why researchers look at the balance between them.

    The see-saw

    Melatonin vs. cortisol

    Their daily rhythms run in opposite directions: cortisol peaks in the morning and melatonin at night. When cortisol stays up into the evening, for example under stress, sleep tends to suffer.
