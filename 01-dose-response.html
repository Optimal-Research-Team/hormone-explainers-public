    Reading the curve

    The curve shows healthy function at each level of cortisol. It peaks in the middle band and falls away on both sides.

    Too little and too much cortisol both cause harm. The two ends of the curve are recognized medical conditions, not everyday stress.

    Too little: deficiency

    • Vasodilation, ↓ blood pressure, dizziness on standing
    • ↓ glucose, ↓ free fatty acids, ↓ amino acids
    • ↑ cytokines, fever; altered immune cells
    • Malaise, fatigue, weakness
    • ↓ Na⁺; ↑ K⁺ when aldosterone is also low, as in Addison’s disease
    • Loss of appetite, weight loss, nausea, vomiting

    This is adrenal insufficiency. It is diagnosed with blood tests and treated by replacing the missing hormone.

    Just right: normal actions

    • Gluconeogenesis and glycogenesis
    • Protein and fat catabolism; ↓ peripheral glucose uptake
    • Vascular tone; Na⁺ / K⁺ balance
    • Suppresses inflammation (IL-1, IL-6, TNF)
    • Modulates immune cells: ↓ lymphocytes and eosinophils, ↑ neutrophils
    • Cognition, memory, mood, circadian rhythm

    Too much: excess

    • Muscle catabolism, proximal myopathy
    • Osteoporosis, fragility fractures, poor calcium absorption
    • Skin fragility, striae, bruising, slow wound healing
    • Memory impairment, anxiety, depression, poor sleep
    • ↓ GnRH, ↓ growth hormone, central hypothyroidism
    • ↑ glucose, insulin resistance, visceral fat, high blood pressure, atherosclerosis

    This is Cushing’s syndrome, most often caused by long-term steroid medicines. It is diagnosed with specific blood, urine or late-night saliva tests.

    Aim for the middle band

    The body needs a middle band, where cortisol does its everyday work of regulating energy, inflammation, blood pressure and mood.

    Everyday stress moves cortisol up and down within this band. Sleep, daily rhythm and stress load all shape where you sit in it.
