    08 · Where to start

    The top layer gets the marketing

    Marketing often suggests the top layer is the answer. In practice, the basics come first.

    So we build this pyramid the other way: from the base up.

    Layer 1 · the base

    Sleep and circadian rhythm recovery

    A dark, quiet bedroom that feels comfortably cool, with bedding you can adjust: overheating disrupts sleep more than mild cool does. Around 18–20 °C suits many people; older adults may sleep best a little warmer. WHO guidance suggests keeping background noise to about 30 dB, roughly a whisper.

    Good sleep ties to cortisol, insulin resistance, mood, energy and cognition. Morning daylight and dim evenings anchor the body clock that sets the daily cortisol rhythm.

    Start here
    Layer 2

    Exercise, basics, mindfulness, social connection

    The best exercise is the kind you’ll keep doing. Over a week, aim for about 150 minutes of moderate activity plus muscle-strengthening at least twice.

    Basics: hydration, fibre, and enough magnesium, B12, iron and folate, ideally from food, with testing if a deficiency is suspected. Two exceptions: Health Canada advises everyone over 50 to take a daily 400 IU vitamin D supplement, and anyone who could become pregnant to take a multivitamin with 0.4 mg of folic acid.

    Mindfulness practice and social connection can reduce perceived stress and support better sleep.

    Layer 3

    Eating regularly; nutrient deficiencies; insulin resistance

    Regular meals help keep energy and blood sugar steady; long fasts can raise cortisol, so they don’t suit everyone. If you take diabetes medicines, are pregnant or have had an eating disorder, don’t fast without medical advice.

    Correct any deficiencies and address insulin resistance: food and activity first, then prescription medicines such as metformin or GLP-1 medicines if you have prediabetes, type 2 diabetes or obesity (discuss with a clinician).

    Layer 4

    Exercise type and timing; calories and macros

    Once routines are stable, fine-tuning exercise and shifting effort to a diet or meal plan can bring further benefit.

    Layer 5 · the top

    Specialized and combination supplements; exercise programs

    Most useful, if at all, once the layers below are in place. Evidence for many supplements is limited, and some interact with medicines.

    Build from the base

    In this framework, the foundations sit at the bottom and the most marketed options at the top. Start with sleep and circadian rhythm, then move up as each layer becomes routine.
